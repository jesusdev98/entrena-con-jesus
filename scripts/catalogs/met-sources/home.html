About

History

First developed in 1989, the Compendium of Physical Activities was created to provide a standardized way of measuring and classifying physical activities, allowing researchers and health professionals to assess the energy expenditure and health benefits associated with different activities. For a more detailed history, please read: A brief history of the Compendium of Physical Activities

2024 Update

The 2024 Adult Compendium Update provides a 3rd update and expansion the compendium (published in 1993, 2000, and 2011) while introducing a new Compendium for Older Adults and an updated Compendium of Wheelchair Activities.

Our Team

Led by Barbara E. Ainsworth, PhD, MPH, our team is comprised of a diverse team of experienced researchers who are passionate about health and wellness. We are committed to providing a comprehensive list of the energy cost of physical activities to support research, physical activity, and health.

Using the Compendium

  • Researchers may download the compendium for use in their research.
  • The Adult, Older Adult, and Wheelchair Compendia are free to use for commercial purposes.
  • Teachers may use the compendium or portions thereof for educational purposes with their students.
  • Please do not change MET values or combine activities with different MET levels.
  • Please cite the Compendium website or publication(s). Websites may link to the compendium by linking to this page: https://pacompendium.com/.
  • Those with questions about use of the Compendium for these or other purposes should e-mail our team at: compendiumpa@gmail.com. 
  • Language Translations: Translated versions of the 2024 and 2011 Compendia are available here: https://pacompendium.com/translations/

Definition of Terms used in the Adult Compendium

MET (Metabolic Equivalent): The ratio of the work metabolic rate to the resting metabolic rate. One MET is defined as 1 kcal/kg/hour and is roughly equivalent to the energy cost of sitting quietly. A MET also is defined as oxygen uptake in ml/kg/min with one MET equal to the oxygen cost of sitting quietly, equivalent to 3.5 ml/kg/min.

5-Digit Code: Adult Compendium activities are classified by a 5-digit code that identifies the category (heading) as the first 2 digits and type (description) of activity as the last three digits.

Example:

Code    Major Heading   Specific Activity Description

17190    17 – Walking      190 – Walking, 2.8 to 3.4 mph, level, moderate pace, firm surface< 10 mph, bicycling to work or for pleasure (Taylor code 115)
